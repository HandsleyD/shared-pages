Tactics & Style of Play by Team

Celtic 4-3-3 (shifting to 3-5-2 or 4-4-2?)

Martin O'Neill started the season in a familiar 4-3-3 but is actively considering switching shape. The problem: two summer signings — Colombia winger Camilo Durán (£10m) and Danish striker Kasper Hogh (£7m), a combined £17m purchase — don't fit together comfortably in a 4-3-3.

Duran is ineffective from the right flank in that system, while Hogh struggles as a lone centre-forward. O'Neill has teased moving to a 3-5-2 or 4-4-2 diamond to unlock the Durán–Hogh partnership, which he's already compared to the legendary Henrik Larsson–Chris Sutton strike force.

Key observation: O'Neill likened Durán's playing style to Daizen Maeda — high intensity, pressing from the front. Camilo Durán inspired Celtic's 3-0 win over Aberdeen in particular. Benjamin Nygren remains their assist leader and creative hub in midfield.

Rangers 4-2-3-1 / 3-5-2

Rangers look the sharpest of any team coming into this season. They started with a hard 1-1 draw at Dundee United (their only result before their five-game winning streak), then found their rhythm completely.

The marquee signing was Lawrence Shankland, who left Hearts for £9.5m and heads the betting to be the league's top scorer. He played well early on, but suffered a hamstring injury during the 1-0 win over Motherwell — expected out until 2027. Cameron Kelsy has been warned he must step up as a £9.5m-level striker.

Ryan Naderi has emerged as Rangers' clutch performer: scored against Celtic at Celtic Park (no away fans present due to ban), then came off the bench in the Motherwell game to score the winner when Shankland was forced off. A statement signing on a temporary basis.

Hearts 4-3-3 / 4-2-3-1 (Vrancken style)

The biggest overachievers this season. New manager Wouter Vrancken inherited a team written off before kickoff. He started with three consecutive losses — including a capitulation at Aberdeen despite controlling the game — but since then, a complete turnaround.

Vrancken's Hearts play a high-pressing, possession-based system that forces mistakes. The style is reminiscent of his Ajax DNA — quick transitions, aggressive pressing, and building from the back. Their home record has been perfect (3 wins from 3 games, 9 pts).

The highlight: a crushing 3-1 victory at Hibernian in the Edinburgh derby, their first win in that fixture under Vrancken. They've gone seven matches unbeaten across all competitions since the opening losses. If this continues, another Hearts shock is very possible.

Dundee United New style: 4-3-3 build-up

Tactical transformation under Jim Goodwin. The Terrors have completely changed their identity this season. Where they previously relied on direct play, Goodwin has mandated short passing, playing out from the back, and choreographed midfield rotations.

Summer signing Dylan Tait (from Falkirk) is the tactical linchpin at the heart of this new approach. The opening-day 1-1 draw with Rangers showed their ambition — they actually drew with one of the league's best teams using their new style.

Caveat: This represents a massive philosophical shift and carries risk against more physical opponents.

Aberdeen 4-4-2 / 3-5-2 (McInnes)

Stephen Robinson has left, Derek McInnes takes the reins. The Dons showed resilience early — beating Hearts 2-1 in an opening-day drama despite being outplayed — but have struggled to sustain momentum. Their defensive record (8 conceded in 7) is solid but their attack has lacked a cutting edge with just 7 goals.

Dundee 5-3-2 / 3-4-3 (Pressley)

Massive improvement on last season. Head coach Steven Pressley has them competitive — they were competitive at Celtic Park for large spells. But Pressley admits the squad is four players short of what he needs. The aim is to add quality loan players in forward areas to replicate last term's success with Cameron Congreve, Yan Dhanda, and Tony Yogane.

St Mirren 4-2-3-1

Another overachiever. Excellent first-half form — best home PPG at the mid-table cut. Killian Phillips has been their talisman: top scorer in the league so far, already in the golden-boot conversation after just 7 games. They had a slight dip recently (3 losses in last 4) but remain competitive with 10 points.

Falkirk Traditional 4-4-2

Classic second-season syndrome. After guiding the Bairns to a top-six finish on promotion last season, John McGlynn faces a much harder task this year. The loss of key performers like Barney Stewart, Calvin Miller, Filip Lissah and Tait has been felt badly. They've shown their promoted side can still compete (drawing Dundee Utd at home), but the gap is closing.

Overachievers So Far

🔥 Hearts — 3rd, 13 pts. New manager Wouter Vrancken has them playing like title challengers. Three straight league losses to start followed by a complete turnaround. Perfect home record (9/9), seven matches unbeaten across all competitions. Their first Edinburgh derby win under Vrancken was crushing: 3-1 away at Hibernian. This is the "another Hearts shock" storyline that pundits predicted — and they're making it real.
🔥 Dundee — 4th, 10 pts. Were the bookies' favourites for relegation just 12 months ago. Now competitive at Celtic Park and sitting firmly mid-table with more than a point per game. Steven Pressley's work is impressive given he describes his squad as four players short of what they need. Tough run ahead — this might not last, but what an improvement already.
🔥 St Mirren — 5th, 10 pts. Excellent first-half form before a slight dip. Killian Phillips has been phenomenal — top scorer in the league. They were promotion favourites on paper but over-delivering so far with their best home PPG at the mid-table cut.
⚠️ Dundee United — tactical gamble paying off. The complete philosophical shift under Jim Goodwin (from direct to possession-based) is risky in Scotland's physical league, but Dylan Tait is making it work so far. A 1-1 draw with Rangers using a new style says a lot. Keep watching this one.

Special Storylines This Season

🚫 Old Firm away fans banned. Celtic and Rangers are both banned from sending away supporters to Old Firm derbies this season following crowd trouble at the Scottish Cup quarter-final at Ibrox in March. The matches have been played behind closed doors with dozens of arrests made after fan clashes. This is a significant security situation for one of world football's most famous rivalries.
🔄 Celtic management uncertainty. Martin O'Neill won the title last season but reports suggest he may leave Celtic at the end of this campaign. The club is reportedly already in talks with potential replacements — including a "kamikaze" 4-3-3 specialist manager (likely referring to a Scandinavian coach). A turbulent off-season backdrop to an on-pitch title defence.
📊 Aberdeen managerial change. Stephen Robinson has departed, with Derek McInnes appointed as the new manager. The early results (8 pts from 7) suggest a transitional period, but beating Hearts in opening day drama showed his side can still produce big-game results.
🏆 Title conversation: three-horse race? While Celtic and Rangers are the clear favourites (18 and 16 pts), Hearts' home form and Vrancken's tactical influence have put them genuinely in the conversation. If they can keep their home record perfect, a third contender is possible — a genuine "three-horse race" which hasn't been common in recent Scottish Premiership history.
