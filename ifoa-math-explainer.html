IFoA Actuarial Mathematics

A Roadmap of Mathematical Competencies

Becoming an actuary requires transitioning from "calculating" to "modelling." The IFoA curriculum progresses from foundational calculus and probability to complex stochastic processes and statistical inference.

CORE FOUNDATION

1. Calculus & Algebra

The language of change and accumulation. You need this to model continuous cash flows and optimize risk.

Key Concepts:

  • Differentiation: Finding rates of change and local maxima/minima (critical for optimization).
  • Integration: Calculating areas under curves—essential for finding probabilities and continuous present values.
  • Series: Summing infinite sequences (e.g., geometric series for perpetuities).
Continuous Probability Density: P(a < X < b) = ∫[a to b] f(x) dx
Example: Finding the expected value of a continuous variable: E[X] = ∫ x · f(x) dx.

2. Probability & Statistics

The core of modern actuarial science. This is how we handle uncertainty and draw conclusions from data.

Key Concepts:

  • Probability Distributions: Discrete (Poisson, Binomial) and Continuous (Normal, Exponential, Gamma).
  • Statistical Inference: Parameter estimation (MLE, Method of Moments) and Hypothesis Testing.
  • Regression: Modeling the relationship between variables (e.g., age vs. mortality rate).
Bayes' Theorem: P(A|B) = [ P(B|A) · P(A) ] / P(B)
Example: Using a Poisson distribution to model the number of insurance claims in a month.
CM1 & CM2

3. Actuarial Mathematics

Applying probability and calculus to the time value of money and life contingencies.

Key Concepts:

  • Financial Mathematics: Interest rates (nominal vs. effective), annuities, and bond pricing.
  • Life Contingencies: Modeling survival probabilities and the present value of future life benefits.
  • Loss Models: Frequency and severity modeling for general insurance.
Present Value Factor: v = 1 / (1 + i) Annuity immediate: a_n| = [1 - (1 + i)^-n] / i
Example: Calculating the premium for a life insurance policy based on the expected present value of future claims.
SP & SA

4. Advanced Stochastic Modelling

At the specialist level, you deal with time-dependent randomness and complex risk structures.

Key Concepts:

  • Stochastic Processes: Markov Chains, Brownian Motion, and Poisson Processes.
  • Time Series: ARIMA models and volatility modeling.
  • Risk Theory: Ruin theory and aggregate loss distributions.
Markov Transition: P(X_n+1 = j | X_n = i) = p_ij
Example: Using a Markov chain to model a policyholder moving between "Healthy", "Ill", and "Deceased" states.
